1. From sightings to a modelling problem

The raw material is not laboratory data. It is civilian reporting: short summaries, longer descriptions, dates, locations, shapes, durations, and NUFORC editorial metadata. That makes the modelling question deliberately careful.

The goal is not to prove what happened in the sky. The goal is to understand how people report anomalous observations at scale.

2. Text cleaning and topic features

The topic-modelling notebooks showed a useful failure mode: naive topic models are dominated by words like light, bright, and sky. Those words describe the whole corpus, so they are weak at separating one kind of report from another.

Cleaning

Normalize text, remove high-frequency sighting vocabulary, test unigrams and n-grams, and keep topic features that survive incidence filtering.

Topic models

LDA acts as an interpretable baseline. HDP is the stronger fit for exploration because it does not force a fixed number of topics upfront.

The output of this stage is the bridge artifact: features_and_topics.pickle, used by the clustering notebooks as the structured-plus-semantic feature source.

3. Semantic embeddings

BERT-style embeddings complement topic models. Topic models capture recurring word groups; embeddings capture semantic similarity even when witnesses use different wording.

The exploratory page uses this to reveal neighborhoods: ordinary light reports, structured craft descriptions, MADAR node logs, editorial notes, and more unusual narrative accounts.

4. Structured feature engineering

The final clustering model combines text-derived features with structured signals from place, time, shape, and duration.

def encode_cyclic_hour(hour):
    angle = 2 * np.pi * hour / 24
    return np.sin(angle), np.cos(angle)

# 23:00 and 00:00 become neighbors on the unit circle,

5. Dimensionality reduction before clustering

DBSCAN is distance-based, and high-dimensional sparse features make distance thresholds brittle. The clustering experiments compare PCA, FastICA, and UMAP before fitting density clusters.

PCA

A disciplined linear baseline that preserves global variance.

FastICA

A baseline for non-Gaussian independent components.

UMAP

The most useful public view because it preserves local neighborhoods and makes clusters inspectable.

DBSCAN

Finds dense groups while leaving unusual reports as noise rather than forcing every report into a class.

6. Bayesian hyperparameter tuning

DBSCAN has two sensitive knobs: eps, the neighborhood radius, and min_samples, the density threshold. A grid search wastes trials because most combinations produce all noise, one giant cluster, or many tiny fragments.

Bayesian optimization is a better fit: it uses the history of previous trials to choose more promising parameters, while the objective function rejects degenerate clusterings.

def score_dbscan(labels, X):
    n_clusters = count_non_noise_clusters(labels)
    noise_share = np.mean(labels == -1)

    if n_clusters < 2 or noise_share > 0.80:
        return penalty

    return silhouette_score(X[labels != -1], labels[labels != -1])

7. Turning clusters into readable profiles

The final interpretability layer is delta-variance profiling. For each cluster, compare feature behavior inside the group to the whole dataset. The largest differences become the cluster fingerprint.

Readable labelEvidence to use
Recent light/orb reportsShape distribution, year distribution, summary snippets.
Structured craft descriptionsDisk/cigar/triangle shape rates, daytime signal, representative text.
MADAR or instrument logsTopic terms, short automated summaries, outlier status.
Editorial or low-confidence reportsNUFORC note language, HOAX??, unusual source markers.

This keeps the clusters honest: a label only earns its place if the feature profile and real snippets agree.

8. Limitations and next steps

The analysis is intentionally careful. NUFORC reports are self-submitted and culturally mediated. Reporting volume reflects access, attention, and ease of submission. Topic models and embeddings discover language patterns, not ground truth.
